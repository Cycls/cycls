# video-direction: the story and the look, before the first write

The contract makes a video correct, not worth watching. Left alone, every video comes out the same: a dark or
cream ground, small centred text, everything fading up. Before the first `write`, take the decisions below in
your head (no message, no extra call), write the plan in §2 for yourself, then write the video once.

## 1. The brief in one breath

- **The one thing**: what the viewer knows, feels or does at the end, in the viewer's words ("my coffee is
  ready when I arrive"), not the slogan.
- **Where it plays**: Reels, TikTok, Stories → vertical, often with the sound off; a feed → square or portrait;
  YouTube, a website, a screen → wide. Several places → one file with data-sizes.
- **Length**: a reel 8–20 s, an announcement 10–15 s, an explainer 30–60 s; a voice-over sets it. A length the
  person gives is a range, never padded.
- **Facts vs choices**: every claim, number, price and date on screen comes from the person or their files.
  Invent only the look and sample content; never derive a number they did not give.
- Ask at most one question, only when the message itself is missing. Otherwise decide, and name your choices
  in one line of the reply (look, length, sizes, sound).

## 2. The story

**The hook (0–2 s).** Frame 0 is the thumbnail: the ground, the carrier and the first words are already there
(never an empty frame, never the logo alone); the headline reads by 0.6 s. Open on the outcome in the viewer's
words, a moment that creates the need, a question with the real input in it, or the result itself. Never
"Introducing…", "Meet…" or the logo first. With the sound off, the words carry the hook.

**A device and a form.** Sketch three one-line storylines (a device + a form), one bolder than your first idea,
and build the one a stranger would follow. When the person asks for options, or an open brief matters, offer the
three in one short message (each: its first 2 s and its look) and wait; otherwise build yours and name the other
two in one line of the reply.
- Devices: the result first · a moment in someone's day · before / after · one real number made physical ·
  type carries the argument · a question with the input in it · one object travels through every scene.
- Forms: one continuous take (the camera pushes from one world into the next) · object to object (each scene
  ends on the thing that opens the next) · cut on the beat · one frame that rebuilds itself · split screen
  collapsing to one. Plain cuts between unrelated scenes is not a form.

**Beats** (15–40 s; under 10 s: hook → payoff → end card):

| Beat | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 10–15% | the need or the result, already moving |
| Turn | ~10% | the old way failing, or the question |
| Reveal | ~10% | the product or idea named, 20–30% in |
| Proof | ~35% | 2–4 concrete things, one a beat, each with its verb |
| Payoff | ~15% | the biggest visual moment, once, 60–80% in |
| End card | 10–15% | the mark carried in, one line, the call to action; holds still ≥1.5 s |

**Rhythm.** Name it ("hook-PUNCH-proof-proof-PAYOFF-end"). The shortest scene 1–1.5 s, the longest 3–4.5 s;
something new every 2–4 s; nothing frozen over 3 s except the end card. In a scene: build (first 30%), breathe
(one slow move), resolve (land, then hold). With music, choose the track before the plan and write the plan in
its beats: the `music` reply gives the beat (B), and starting the track at one of its good places puts a beat at
0 and every B after it. Each scene is a whole number of beats (4 beats at B = 0.492 → 1.97 s), the payoff and the
end card land on a bar line (every 4 beats), and the plan's seconds are those beat times.

**Every element gets a verb** from the beat's idea: SLAMS, STAMPS, DROPS · SLIDES, PUSHES, WIPES · DRAWS,
FILLS, COUNTS UP, ASSEMBLES · FLOATS, DRIFTS · TYPES ON, SNAPS, LOCKS IN. No verb: not designed yet.

**Copy.** At most 6 words a title line, one line read at a time, no full stop on titles. The lines alone tell
the story; so do the pictures. Never "unlock", "seamless", "supercharge", "game-changer", "effortless". Arabic
copy is written as Arabic, not translated.

**The plan you write for yourself** (in your thinking):

```
ONE THING: قهوتك جاهزة قبل ما توصل (the order is ready when you arrive)
LOOK: Sadu weave: sand #e8d9bf, ink #3a2214, accent #9b2226 (one word a scene + the band), support #1f5c45;
      Amiri 700 200px / Readex Pro 400 60px; woven band 190px at y 1500; grain 9%
RHYTHM: hook-PROOF-end, 13.5 s, silent
| # | s       | beat  | on screen (≤6 words)       | hero move                      | in                |
| 1 | 0–2.6   | hook  | قهوتك جاهزة / قبل ما توصل   | line 2 RISES right to left     | -                 |
| 2 | 2.6–5.0 | proof | arch window: the roaster    | window WIPES up, photo PUSHES  | cut, band runs on |
| 3 | 5.0–7.0 | end   | سنا / اطلبها من التطبيق      | mark STAMPS, then holds        | fade 0.4          |
```

## 3. Looks

A look is the rules of one medium: what the ground is made of, what carries text, what the one accent may
touch, and how the medium moves (print stamps, paper is cut and lifted, wool weaves, film pushes in, gold
catches the light). Pick by the feeling and the occasion before the industry: a bank's Ramadan video is a Gulf
look in the bank's colours. A campaign shares one look; different clients should not.

1. **Sadu weave**: Founding and National Day, hospitality, coffee, heritage. Sand `#e8d9bf`, ink `#3a2214`,
   red `#9b2226`, palm `#1f5c45`. Amiri 700 + Readex Pro 400 (Latin: Playfair Display + DM Sans). A woven band
   (G2) on grained sand, one word in red. Words rise in reading order, 0.7–0.9 s; the band slides under every
   scene as the carrier. fade, light-leak · accent-wave, accent-sparkles in `#b08d57` · middle-eastern.
2. **Night and gold leaf**: Ramadan, Eid, weddings, invitations, luxury, real estate. Navy `#0b1d3a`, cream
   `#f5ecd7`, gold `#d4af37`. Amiri 700; Playfair Display 500 and italic; Montserrat 500 caps 30 px, .3em. A sky
   with a crescent (G3), a gold hairline arch, a sheen across the gold once. Blur-ins 0.8–1 s, the arch rises,
   long holds. light-leak, fade · accent-sparkle in gold · minimal-piano, calm.
3. **Print poster**: sales, launches, sport, events. One loud ground (`#ff4d00`, `#ffd60a`, `#1f3fff`), ink
   `#111111`, a white plate. Montserrat 900 caps 170–260 px, −0.03em, line-height .92; Arabic Cairo or Tajawal
   900, 15% larger. Flat planes, a giant numeral cut by the edge, a plate off register (G4). STAMPS: 0.3–0.4 s on
   a spring, hard cuts. whip-pan, flash-through-white · accent-burst, accent-bolt, accent-confetti · upbeat.
4. **Swiss print**: results, B2B, conferences, data. `#f2f2ef`, ink `#111111`, red `#e63322`. Inter 900 + 400,
   tabular numbers; Arabic IBM Plex Sans Arabic 700/400, flush right. A 90 px hairline grid (2 px lines, ink at
   10%, wiped down), everything flush to one edge, numbers huge. Rules draw, numbers count 1.2–1.6 s, words wipe on `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175,
   1)`. cuts, one cinematic-zoom · accent-check, accent-plus · corporate, tech.
5. **Editorial page**: stories, food, culture, case studies. Paper `#f3ece0`, ink `#1e1a16`, terracotta
   `#b4532a`, olive `#5f6f52`. DM Serif Display + DM Sans; Arabic Amiri + IBM Plex Sans Arabic. Grain, a
   hard-edged photo, a pull quote, rules; lines draw, words wipe. fade · accent-underline, accent-circle ·
   warm-acoustic. Only for an editorial subject: as a default it is the most generated look there is.
6. **Cut paper**: beauty, wellness, kids, family, everyday apps. Blush `#fbeee8`, ink `#3d2c2e`, coral
   `#e8735a`, sage `#a8c5b0`, butter `#f6d77a`. Poppins 700/400; Arabic Almarai 800/300. Circles and pills with a
   hard paper shadow (`0 22px 0 rgba(61,44,46,.12)`), cut by the edge, popping in on POP (§7), layers drifting at
   different speeds. fade,
   ripple-waves · accent-heart, an emoji · playful, warm-acoustic.
7. **Product studio**: apps, SaaS, AI, fintech. Light sweep `#f4f5f2`, ink `#101418`, the product's colour (or
   cobalt `#2952ff`). Space Grotesk 700 + Inter; Arabic Readex Pro 600 + IBM Plex Sans Arabic. The real screen in
   a rounded frame with a soft contact shadow; the camera pushes into what matters (scale 1 → 1.5 on a wrapper).
   cinematic-zoom, whip-pan · accent-check, accent-arrow · tech. Never draw a fake dashboard to fill a frame.
8. **Documentary film**: people on footage, NGOs, testimonials. The footage, ink `#f5f5f0`, yellow `#f2c94c`.
   Montserrat 800 caps names + Open Sans; Arabic Tajawal 800 + IBM Plex Sans Arabic. A bottom scrim, vignette,
   grain, a lower third growing from its rule (G5); letterbox bars on wide. Cut on speech, stills push 1 → 1.06.
   cuts, fade · inspiring, calm.
9. **Neon sign**: nightlife, gaming, concerts, late openings. Wall `#120d1a`, tubes `#ff2e88` and `#2de2e6`,
   text `#fff3fb`. Space Grotesk 700; Arabic Readex Pro 700. Tubes on a wall (G6), the one look where glow
   belongs; flicker on, lines draw, 0.2–0.3 s. glitch, chromatic-split · accent-bolt, accent-pulse · upbeat.
10. **Annual report**: banks, government, HR, executives. White, navy `#0b1f3a`, blue `#1d4ed8`, surface
   `#e8eef7`. Montserrat 800 + Open Sans; Arabic Tajawal 800/400. A colour band behind the title, real photos,
   counters and bars; bands wipe, numbers count, 0.5–0.7 s. fade, one sdf-iris · accent-check · corporate.

## 4. The brand kit wins

When the guide names a kit: its colours by role (background, text, accent; accent2 as the support), its fonts
by the exact family named, its logo. Choose the look whose medium fits the feeling and recolour it with the kit;
the medium, the composition and the motion stay the look's. The logo lands once, with weight, usually on the
end card, carried in (wiped, stamped, a line drawing into it): never docked in a corner the whole video, never
the first frame. A light logo needs a dark ground or a plate.

## 5. Composing a frame

- The hero fills at least 40% of the frame's height or width; a headline 60–85% of the width. A small thing in
  an empty field is not a shot: push in, crop, or give it a world.
- One focal point leads, one supports; size beats contrast beats colour beats position.
- Anchor to an edge or a band (a headline sitting on the woven band, a name on its rule). Centring is for
  ceremony: an arch, an invitation, an end card.
- Three layers, no more: the ground with its texture moving slowly, the content, one accent.
- One hero move a scene; supporting moves travel 50–70% as far and start 100–200 ms later; the rest holds.
- Vertical (1080×1920): text stays between y 220 and y 1440 and out of the right 140 px, where the platforms
  put their names, captions and buttons. Decoration may go there.
- Arabic: dir="rtl" on the scene, anchored right, motion mirrored: words arrive right to left, a wipe opens from
  the right (`clip-path: inset(0 0 0 100%)` → `inset(0 0 0 0)`), a rule draws from `transform-origin: 100% 50%`.
- Continuity: the ground and the carrier (a band, a rule, a colour plane) stay in place across scenes, so cuts
  feel like one move; change the ground only to change chapter.

## 6. Type for video

| | reel / story | square, portrait | wide |
|---|---|---|---|
| hero word or number | 180–360 px | 150–300 | 160–300 |
| headline | 110–200 | 96–160 | 90–150 |
| body, proof line | 48–64 | 44–56 | 40–52 |
| label (caps) | 28–36 | 28–32 | 26–32 |

- Extreme weight contrast: 300–400 against 800–900. Two families at most: a display serif with a sans, or one
  family at both ends; never two similar sans.
- Latin display −0.02 to −0.04em, line-height 0.92–1.05; caps labels +0.12 to +0.3em. Arabic: never tracked,
  line-height 1.5–1.6, about 12% larger than Latin.
- Weights that exist: Amiri 400/700; DM Serif Display 400; Readex Pro and IBM Plex Sans Arabic to 700; Almarai
  to 800; Cairo, Tajawal, Inter, Montserrat, Poppins, Playfair Display to 900.
- Arabic display without vowels: at 200 px and up a shadda or damma floats off and reads as a letter. Vowels
  belong in the voice-over script and in small text where a word may be misread.
- Counters in `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, the unit at about 45% size in the accent; one kind of digits
  a video. A Latin name inside Arabic: `<span dir="ltr">`.
- Text lands, then holds: it never drifts or shrinks while it is read (the ground may move).

## 7. Grounds (checked on the renderer)

Decorative text (ghost words, giant numerals, plates) carries `data-layout-ignore aria-hidden="true"`; shapes and
pushed photos reaching past the frame carry `data-layout-allow-overflow`; without them the checks fail them.
`POP` below is `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`, a small overshoot.

- **G1 Grain**: `.grain { position: absolute; inset: 0; opacity: .09; mix-blend-mode: multiply; background-image:
  repeating-radial-gradient(circle at 17% 32%, #3a2214 0 1px, transparent 1px 3px), repeating-radial-gradient(circle
  at 71% 64%, #3a2214 0 1px, transparent 1px 4px); }` (on a dark ground `overlay` with `#fff`). Film grain is
  `inset: -10px` and jitters:
  ```js
  at("#grain", Array.from({ length: 31 }, (_, i) => ({ transform: `translate(${(i * 37) % 7 - 3}px, ${(i * 53) % 7 - 3}px)` })), 0, 2500, "steps(30, end)");
  ```
- **G2 Sadu band**, sliding like a loom; the next scene's band starts where this one stopped:
  ```html
  <div class="sadu"><div class="st" style="top:14px"></div><div id="zig" class="zig"></div><div class="st" style="top:160px"></div></div>
  ```
  ```css
  .sadu { position: absolute; left: 0; right: 0; top: 1500px; height: 190px; background: #1a1410; }
  .sadu .st { position: absolute; left: 0; right: 0; height: 16px; background: #9b2226; }
  .sadu .zig { position: absolute; left: 0; right: 0; top: 46px; height: 80px; background-color: #f2e6cf; background-size: 80px 80px;
    background-image: linear-gradient(135deg, #9b2226 25%, transparent 25%), linear-gradient(225deg, #9b2226 25%, transparent 25%),
                      linear-gradient(315deg, #9b2226 25%, transparent 25%), linear-gradient(45deg, #9b2226 25%, transparent 25%); }
  ```
  `at("#zig", [{ backgroundPosition: "-40px 0, -40px 0, 0 0, 0 0" }, { backgroundPosition: "-248px 0, -248px 0, -208px 0, -208px 0" }], 0, 2600, "linear");`
- **G3 Night and a gold arch**: navy under one soft glow, `radial-gradient(60% 38% at 72% 16%,
  rgba(245,236,215,.16), transparent 70%)`; stars as 6–10 layers of `radial-gradient(3px 3px at 12% 14%, #f5ecd7 50%,
  transparent 52%)`; a crescent: a 190 px circle with `border-radius: 50%; box-shadow: -38px 18px 0 0 #f5ecd7`; an
  arch: `border: 4px solid #d4af37; border-bottom: 0; border-radius: 390px 390px 0 0; overflow: hidden`, rising with
  clipPath `inset(100% 0 0 0)` → `inset(0 0 0 0)`, a 260 px sheen band inside it (`mix-blend-mode: screen;
  linear-gradient(100deg, transparent, rgba(255,236,190,.35), transparent)`) crossing once. An arch holding an
  `<img>` is a window onto a photo.
- **G4 Print plate and giant numeral**: the plate is a white copy of the headline under the ink, nudged off register.
  ```html
  <div class="ghost" data-layout-ignore aria-hidden="true">50</div>
  <div class="poster"><div id="plate" class="plate" data-layout-ignore aria-hidden="true">SALE ENDS<br>SUNDAY</div><div class="ink">SALE ENDS<br>SUNDAY</div></div>
  ```
  `.ghost { position: absolute; right: -260px; top: 120px; font: 900 1100px/1 "Montserrat"; color: rgba(17,17,17,.13); white-space: nowrap; }`,
  `.poster { position: absolute; left: 70px; top: 560px; font: 900 190px/.92 "Montserrat"; letter-spacing: -0.03em; }`,
  `.ink { position: relative; color: #111111; } .plate { position: absolute; left: 0; top: 0; color: #ffffff; }`, and
  `at("#plate", [{ opacity: 0, transform: "translate(0, 0)" }, { opacity: 1, transform: "translate(9px, 7px)" }], 450, 300, "cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)");`
- **G5 Photo under a scrim**: `<div id="shot" data-layout-allow-overflow><img src="uploads/photo.jpg"></div>` (both
  `position: absolute; inset: 0`, the img `width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: cover`) pushing scale 1 → 1.07 across the scene, a scrim
  `linear-gradient(to bottom, rgba(12,12,12,0) 40%, rgba(12,12,12,.88) 80%)` with the text on its dark end, and a
  vignette `radial-gradient(ellipse at center, transparent 55%, rgba(0,0,0,.55))`.
- **G6 Neon**: `color: #fff3fb; text-shadow: 0 0 6px #ff2e88, 0 0 22px #ff2e88, 0 0 60px rgba(255,46,136,.7)`, lit by a
  flicker whose keyframes each step:
  ```js
  const s = "steps(1, end)";
  at("#neon", [{ opacity: 0, easing: s }, { opacity: 1, offset: .1, easing: s }, { opacity: .15, offset: .18, easing: s },
    { opacity: 1, offset: .3, easing: s }, { opacity: .5, offset: .38, easing: s }, { opacity: 1, offset: .46 }, { opacity: 1 }], 100, 1200, "linear");
  ```
  A tube draws as an SVG path with `stroke-linecap="round" pathLength="100" stroke-dasharray="100 120"
  stroke-dashoffset="110"`, `filter: drop-shadow(0 0 10px #2de2e6)`, strokeDashoffset 110 → 0.

## 8. The defaults that make a video look generated

Avoid each one unless the brand or the look's own medium uses it: a black or navy void with small things
floating in the middle · glow, orbs, particles, lens flares as decoration · purple-to-blue gradients, mesh
gradients, glass cards (a full-screen linear gradient on a dark ground also bands in the MP4) · cream paper with
a serif, an italic accent word and "01 / 02" labels, by default · everything centred · everything fading and
sliding up alike, every scene entering the same way · text drifting after it lands · corner brackets, frame
borders, HUD readouts as decoration · three cards in a row, a KPI grid, a title over a dimmed screenshot · the
logo first or docked in a corner · gradient text · pure `#000000` or `#ffffff` grounds · an accent under 15%
opacity (it vanishes in compression).

## 9. Look at the sheet as a stranger would

After every `write`, before `render`:
1. Describe each frame in one sentence, naming what is in it. A thing you would have to explain is wrong.
2. The first cell is a finished thumbnail with the hook readable.
3. Cover the words: the pictures tell it. Read only the words: they tell it.
4. Name every default from §8 on screen and replace it from the subject, the brand or the medium.
5. Sizes from §6, nothing in the platform zones, nothing over anything.
6. Every line long enough to read; nothing frozen over 3 s before the end card; the end card holds ≥1.5 s.
7. The accent on one thing a scene.

Fix all of it in ONE `edit`, then `look` at the moments you changed. When a video feels busy, remove a third of
the motion and keep the moves that lead the eye.
